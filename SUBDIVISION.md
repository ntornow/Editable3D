# Subdivision and limit queries

`Subdivision.catmullClark(mesh, levels?, options?)` returns a new refined polygon cage. `Subdivision.multires(mesh, levels, options?)` builds editable refinement levels using the same rules. Both preserve their existing return shapes and leave the input unchanged. The geometric crease and boundary rules are described in [ATTRIBUTES.md](ATTRIBUTES.md). `Subdivision.limit` and `prepareLimit` evaluate geometric limit points without constructing a globally refined mesh. Refinement, data interpolation and limit evaluation are separate operations, as explained in the [OpenSubdiv subdivision and tessellation overview](https://opensubdiv.org/docs/subdivision_surfaces.html#subdivision-versus-tessellation).

## Geometric limit queries

```luau
local evaluator = E.Subdivision.prepareLimit(cage, {
    tolerance = 1e-5,
    boundary = "edge",
    creasing = "chaikin",
    requireComplete = true,
})
local sample = evaluator:evaluate(faceId, 0.27, 0.63)
local position = sample.position
local normal = sample.normal -- may be absent; check availability
local statistics = evaluator:statistics()
```

`Subdivision.limit(mesh, faceId, u, v, options?)` is a single-query convenience. `prepareLimit(mesh, options?)` snapshots positions, topology, geometric creases and scalar options; later source edits do not change that evaluator. No mesh, channel, material, group, skin weight or scene object is modified. Evaluation is geometric: face-varying fields and texture coordinates are not evaluated by these APIs.

A quad's ordered vertices correspond to `(0,0), (1,0), (1,1), (0,1)`. Parameters are finite numbers in `[0,1]`. A nonquad requires an explicit corner patch: pass `options.corner=k` to `limit`, or a fourth argument to `evaluator:evaluate(faceId,u,v,k)`. This selects the first refined quad ordered as the chosen old vertex, following edge point, face point and preceding edge point. Its own parameters range from zero to one. The optional corner form also works on quads. Derivatives refer to the requested patch coordinates, including rotations and factors of two introduced during further subdivision.

Each result has `position`, `errorBound`, `complete`, `success`, `reason`, `method`, `face`, optional `corner`, `depth`, `work` and `tolerance`. Completion means the positional error bound meets the requested tolerance. `reason` is `tolerance`, `maxDepth` or `precision`. Incomplete results retain a candidate position and its bound for inspection. `requireComplete=true` throws instead. Invalid input, allocation/work/query exhaustion and cancellation always throw. The defaults return incomplete numerical results without throwing.

Four evaluation paths share the same geometric rules:

* `bicubic`: a regular interior quad evaluates its sixteen uniform cubic B-spline controls analytically. The result includes `derivativeU`, `derivativeV`, `derivativeErrorU`, `derivativeErrorV` and `derivativesAvailable=true`, when those derivatives fit native coordinates. A nondegenerate tangent plane also supplies `normal` and `normalErrorBound`.
* `bicubicOneSided`: a quad next to a boundary or infinite crease uses an analytic tensor patch when all four vertex sectors have regular rules. A linear reflected control row reproduces the exact boundary masks. Regular interior sectors have four quads, ordinary crease sectors have two quads, and fixed corner sectors have one quad. The result has `oneSided=true` and the same derivative/error fields as `bicubic`. Positions agree across a crease; each incident face supplies its own partials and normal.
* `vertexMask`: stationary smooth all-quad stars use the closed limit mask `(n² P + 4 sum(edge neighbors) + sum(opposite quad corners)) / (n(n+5))`. Infinite two-edge creases use `(4P+N1+N2)/6`; fixed corners retain `P`. Smooth extraordinary stars of valence at least three use the dominant Fourier eigenmodes of their local subdivision matrix for characteristic normals. Constrained one-face fixed sectors and one-/two-face ordinary crease sectors also supply finite face-chart derivatives, mapped through the requested face orientation. These results have `oneSided=true`, `normalSectorFaces`, and `normalMethod` (`fixedCornerSector` or `creaseCharacteristic`). A nonquad neighbor is refined before applying a quad-sector stencil when depth permits. Other extraordinary face-chart derivatives remain unavailable.
* `convexHull`: other queries refine a local neighborhood until regular masks apply or the one-ring control hull around the parameter cell is small enough. The candidate is the cell's bilinear cage point; the largest distance to a one-ring control bounds its distance to the limit point. This also handles infinitely sharp darts, boundaries and crease intersections. These results have no derivative or normal estimate.

A smooth valence-four vertex with four incident quads now also returns finite chart derivatives when a neighboring feature prevents evaluation of the entire query face as one regular patch. Order radial neighbors `E0..E3` from the queried face's outgoing edge and let `Di` be the opposite corner of the quad between `Ei` and `E(i+1)`. The local partials are `[4(E0-E2)+D0-D1-D2+D3]/12` and `[4(E1-E3)+D0+D1-D2-D3]/12`, then mapped through the face-corner orientation and accumulated child-chart Jacobian. These are the two affine half-eigenvalue modes of the ordinary four-face core. Other core and halo modes decay faster, so features at other vertices do not alter this center jet. This path uses `normalMethod="regularVertexJet"`; center sharpness, hard incident edges and nonquad stars do not select it. Zero or collinear partials keep their derivative values with an unavailable normal. The same native range and rounding bounds apply. Eleven tests cover analytic quadratic tensor slopes, nearby convergence, parameter orientation, outer pins, degeneracies, extreme scales, dyadic fixed-boundary queries, snapshots, budgets and native corner conversion.

`derivativesAvailable=false` comes with `derivativeReason`. Every result has `normalAvailable`; false comes with `normalReason`. Normals can still be available from characteristic modes when parameter derivatives are unavailable or collinear. At a rounded one-face boundary corner, the two finite chart partials are collinear; the next Jordan mode determines the normal. If the boundary controls are exactly collinear native inputs, the surviving diagonal mode can determine it instead. An uncertain near-collinearity never selects that exact-identity fallback. Availability is separate from positional completion.

A fixed two-face quad sector also has finite partials when its original native center `P` is **exactly** the midpoint of its two hard boundary neighbors `A` and `B`. This includes explicit vertex sharpness, the `fixed` boundary rule and a qualifying sector at a junction of three or more hard edges. Expansion arithmetic proves `A+B=2P` without rounding away residuals. The fixed and ordinary crease vertex masks then both retain `P`; the hard edge children `(P+A)/2` and `(P+B)/2` preserve the identity at every refinement. The resulting jet uses `normalMethod="fixedMidpointCrease"`, `oneSided=true` and `normalSectorFaces=2`. No subdivision rule changes.

For this case, let `M` be the interior edge neighbor and `D0,D1` the two quad diagonals. In the sector's oriented coordinates, the tangent vectors are `(A-B)/2` and `(4*(M-P)+D0+D1-2P)/6`; the requested face and parameter corner determine their order and signs. The interior-edge/diagonal-sum recurrence has eigenvalues `1/2` and `1/8`, giving finite scaled edge limits. Existing native derivative and normal error bounds apply. Degenerate tangent vectors can still provide finite partials while leaving the normal unavailable. This identity shortcut uses original controls only: uncertain refined controls, nearly equal midpoints and unsupported sector sizes do not qualify. Whole-face tensor-patch recognition is unchanged.

Fixed two-face sectors with noncollinear hard boundary vectors now return `normalMethod="fixedCornerJordan"`. Their oriented limiting normal is parallel to `(A-P):Cross(B-P)`, where A and B are the two boundary neighbors in sector order. Both incident faces have the same orientation. The transverse face-chart partial grows logarithmically, so `derivativesAvailable` remains false even when `normalAvailable` is true. This applies to an explicitly sharp vertex, a fixed boundary vertex, or a two-face sector at a hard-edge junction. Ordinary subdivision masks are unchanged; a limiting normal does not exclude folds farther from the corner.

There is also a proved collinear case. When exact original-coordinate predicates establish that the two nonzero boundary rays point in opposite directions, `normalMethod="fixedCollinearJordan"` uses the oriented plane spanned by `t=(A-B)/2` and `a=(4M+D0+D1-6P)/6`. The exact-midpoint case above still takes precedence and retains its finite derivatives. Unequal opposed rays generally have an unbounded transverse chart partial, despite their well-defined normal. Same-direction, zero-length, uncertain-collinear or degenerate plane cases do not use this extension.

The proof includes the complete annular patch neighborhood, not just the central vertex spectrum. After two local refinements, a two-quad sector has a regular half-grid around the fixed center. The fifteen controls `(i,j)`, `i=-2..2`, `j=0..2`, suffice both for its self-similar subdivision operator T and the six regular bicubic or one-sided patches on a fundamental dyadic annulus. New samples on a fixed boundary ray are exact midpoints, so fixed and crease masks agree there. Exact Fraction arithmetic verifies all 225 operator entries against full-cage subdivision.

Let `s=A+B-2P`, `t=(A-B)/2`, and `a=(4M+D0+D1-6P)/6`. The constant spectral projector copies P. The generalized projector at eigenvalue 1/2 is `X*t+Y*a+W*s`, where X and Y reproduce the linear chart coordinates and W is a finite scalar mode. Its nilpotent part is `Y*s/12` and squares to zero. Every remaining eigenvalue has modulus at most 1/4; an exact residual annihilator verifies this for the whole fifteen-control neighborhood. Thus on each annular patch the rescaled surface has the form `x*t+y*a+(w+k*y/6)*s+e_k`, with `e_k` tending to zero in C1 faster than any polynomial in k. The fixed finite patch-extraction maps make convergence uniform, including the hard boundaries. Consequently `(du cross dv)/k` tends uniformly to `t cross s/6`, equal to `(A-P) cross (B-P)/6`. A nonzero bounded cross product gives the stated normal.

For opposed collinear rays, `s=c*t` with `abs(c)<2`. The exact horizontal differences of W's control rows are bounded by 1/2. Cubic refinement preserves the corresponding slope bound; the linearly reflected one-sided patch also has nonnegative effective transverse weights. Hence `abs(dw/dx)<=1/2` on the full annulus. The limiting cross product is `(t cross a)*(1+c*dw/dx)`, whose scalar factor stays strictly positive. This establishes the second normal without asserting finite partials. The existing conservative native arithmetic and unit-direction error bounds apply to both methods. Numerical noncollinearity or a nonzero plane must dominate its error bound before a normal is returned.

Ordinary three-face crease sectors now provide `normalMethod="creaseThreeSector"` when their two controlling directions are provably nondegenerate. Let `E0..E3` be the four ordered radial neighbors, `D0..D2` the quad diagonals, and `P` the center. With `lambda=(11+sqrt(57))/32`, the directions are `T=(E0-E3)/2` and

```text
V = [(-16-64lambda)P + (18-80lambda)(E0+E3)
     + (12+48lambda)(E1+E2) + (-11+32lambda)(D0+2D1+D2)] / 114
```

The oriented limit normal is parallel to `T cross V`. A full 21-control operator includes the surrounding patch controls. Exact quadratic-field eigenvectors and all 324 strictly positive Bernstein coefficients of the nine annular characteristic Jacobians establish uniform normal convergence, including both hard boundaries. All other nonconstant modes below `lambda` and `1/2` have modulus less than `1/2`. Interval bounds on `lambda` are included in the native normal error.

Since `lambda>1/2`, the transverse chart derivative generally grows under dyadic refinement; `derivativesAvailable` remains false. There is an exact exception: both rational coefficient vectors of `V` can vanish on original native controls. Expansion arithmetic checks this identity before returning finite, possibly degenerate, partials. In sector order, the three face-coordinate pairs are `(T,T)`, `(T,-T)` and `(-T,-T)`, mapped to the requested parameter corner. These rank-one jets do not supply a normal by themselves. A rounded near-zero mode never qualifies.

A fixed three-face sector with the exact original identity `E0+E3=2P` follows the same crease recurrence. It returns `normalMethod="fixedMidpointThreeSector"` under the same normal and finite-jet conditions. This includes qualifying sectors at hard-edge junctions. Other fixed three-face configurations can use the exact annular proof below. `Examples.ThreeFaceCrease` demonstrates the normal query. Fifteen cases verify 882 production matrix entries, both exact eigenprojections, all annular Jacobian signs, analytic patch convergence, midpoint restrictions, finite-mode annihilation, parameter rotations, transforms, snapshots, limits and native normal/UV conversion.

Ordinary Catmull-Clark boundary rules do not guarantee a regular tangent plane at every extraordinary constrained vertex; dominant eigenvalues alone are insufficient. Default queries use the inexpensive stencils above. The optional exact annular proof below extends constrained-sector coverage; infinitely sharp darts and unresolved degeneracies retain explicit unavailable results. Nearby regular patches can still have analytic derivatives. These distinctions are consistent with the boundary-rule problems and characteristic-map requirement discussed by [Biermann, Levin and Zorin](https://cims.nyu.edu/gcl/papers/biermann2000pss.pdf). Face-varying limit evaluation remains separate. `normalErrorBound`, when present, bounds distance between unit directions; it is not an angular value. It does not certify global orientation, injectivity or absence of self-intersections.

`exactConstrainedJets=true` enables a bounded source-specific proof when a stationary constrained quad sector has no existing finite derivative stencil. This covers arbitrary sector counts within the proof budgets, including higher fixed corners and ordinary creases with exactly absent growing modes. The source controls must be exact original native inputs; controls carrying refinement uncertainty return an `uncertainControls` certificate. Default queries keep their existing inexpensive stencils. This option does not change any subdivision mask.

Let the core contain `P`, radial neighbors `E0..En`, and diagonals `D0..D(n-1)`. Its stationary matrix `A` is given by `P'=P` for a corner or `(6P+E0+En)/8` for a crease; hard endpoints satisfy `E0'=(P+E0)/2`, `En'=(P+En)/2`. Interior rays satisfy `Ej'=3(P+Ej)/8+(E(j-1)+E(j+1)+D(j-1)+Dj)/16`, and `Di'=(P+Ei+E(i+1)+Di)/4`. Subtract the exact limit center `C=P` or `(4P+E0+En)/6`. For `B=2A` and the three-coordinate state `w=source-C`, rational elimination finds and independently rechecks the minimal vector Krylov relation `p(B)w=0`.

The scaled recurrence converges precisely when the only active unit-circle root is a simple root at one and every other root has modulus less than one. If present, remove one factor `(z-1)` from `p` to obtain `q`; a second such factor gives `reason="unitJordanMode"`. The exact real Schur reflection recursion proves strict disk stability of `q` (or `p` when there is no unit root). Its ascending-coefficient step uses leading `a`, constant `b`, rejects `b*b>=a*a`, and replaces the polynomial by `(a*p(z)-b*z^degree*p(1/z))/((a*a-b*b)*z)`. See the scalar criterion in [Olshevsky's Schur–Cohn paper, section 0.1.2](https://www2.math.uconn.edu/~olshevsky/papers/schurcohn_s.pdf). A failed strict test gives `reason="noncontractingModes"`. Failure describes the active recurrence; it does not discard a normal already proved by another method.

The exact limit is zero without a unit mode, otherwise `D=q(B)w/q(1)`. The implementation verifies `BD=D`, zero center, `E(j-1)+E(j+1)=0`, and `Di=Ei+E(i+1)` on this limit. These identities reproduce an affine chart in each incident face. The proof includes the full `6n+3`-control annular neighborhood: outside the `2n+2` core, each interior-ray three-control block is `[[3/32,1/64,1/64],[1/16,1/16,0],[1/16,0,1/16]]`, with eigenvalues `1/8,1/16,1/32`; each ordinary hard-boundary block is `[[1/8,0],[1/16,1/16]]` (the `fixed` boundary rule instead gives `[[0,0],[1/16,1/16]]`); and each outer diagonal adds `1/64`. Ordered by those blocks, the halo is lower triangular. Its eigenvalues are all strictly below `1/2`. The affine core extends uniquely to the affine halo, and all other scaled modes decay, including any polynomial Jordan factors. Fixed bicubic extraction maps therefore give uniform C1 convergence on every annular patch and hard boundary. This establishes finite face-chart derivatives; an error-separated nonzero derivative cross product also establishes the oriented normal. Neither result claims global injectivity of the sector.

Successful fallback queries use `normalMethod="exactConstrainedJets"` and attach `constrainedJetCertificate`. It contains the exact minimal polynomial, stable-factor reflection coefficients, exact scaled core limit, proof degree and `finiteCertified`. Rationals use signed little-endian base-`2^24` numerator/denominator limbs. Exact finite derivatives are enclosed before native conversion; native range or precision remains separate from the proof. A zero or rank-one jet does not by itself establish a normal. Existing characteristic normals remain available if the new proof fails. The separate annular normal option below can establish normals when finite chart derivatives diverge or are degenerate.

Additional proof defaults are `maxKrylovDegree=128` (the `2n+2` core size must fit), `maxExactCoefficients=1000000` for jet-only queries (20000000 with either exact normal option), and `maxExactBits=8192`. All are positive finite integers. The coefficient preflight is `12N(N+1)+4(N+1)^2`, where `N=2n+2`; it bounds retained rational slots, not bytes. Exact arithmetic consumes the existing cumulative `maxWork`; bit and allocation exhaustion throw. `checkpoint({stage="constrainedJet",degree,coreSize})` runs at each Krylov degree after source snapshotting, and cancellation is checked throughout arithmetic. Callback errors retain their identity. Twenty cases cover 4,500 independent production-mask coefficients, 25 exact vector recurrences, 96 known-root Schur polynomials, seven halo partitions, full-annulus convergence, rotations, transformations, stored-bit perturbations, snapshotting, proof caps and native normal/UV conversion. `Examples.ConstrainedJets` demonstrates the opt-in query.


`exactConstrainedNormals=true` enables a separate bounded proof whenever an existing constrained-sector stencil supplies no normal. It accepts stationary open quad sectors, including arbitrary sector counts within the budgets, and replays queried local refinements exactly from the original native controls. It does not change subdivision masks or infer finite chart partials from a normal. Explicit corner-patch subdivision, initial polygon conversion and finite-sharpness refinement histories can therefore supply the proof. Closed smooth stars and darts retain their existing paths. The source mesh, feature configuration and option values are snapshotted by the prepared evaluator.

The proof lifts the source through two exact local refinements into the complete `6n+3` annular neighborhood. Initial finite edge/vertex sharpness follows the configured uniform or Chaikin rule. A physically fixed boundary and an interior infinite crease receive separate boundary flags. The exact coordinate recurrence is checked to have only real nonnegative active roots below one after subtracting the limit center. A shared scalar invariant basis verifies both source reconstruction and `A*B=B*M`. Pairwise vector cross products then evolve under the exterior square of this reduced operator.

The full stationary operator is integral after scaling by 64; its exterior square is integral after scaling by 4096. Monic minimal recurrences at that scale have integer coefficients. Modular elimination and Chinese remaindering propose a recurrence, and an exact full-state vector identity verifies it before use. The modular degree bound together with that identity establishes minimality. Polynomial GCD candidates likewise require exact divisibility and a matching modular degree bound. Since all active exterior roots are products of the already certified nonnegative coordinate roots, Bernstein/Descartes sign variations count them exactly. Only the highest root is isolated. The real-rooted restriction is essential; see [Peña, “On Descartes’ rules of signs and their exactness”](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1002/mana.200310335). General polynomial queries retain their existing Sturm method.

For dominant root `rho` of multiplicity `m`, applying `p(M)/(M-rho)` to the initial exterior state gives its leading `rho^k*k^(m-1)` direction multiplied by a positive scalar. Exact algebraic evaluation maps this state to the 324 scalar Bernstein coefficients of the three bidegree-five derivative cross products on the queried face's fundamental annuli. Exact output-resolvent cancellation removes unobserved poles before a lower mode is selected. A rational Bezout filter may first remove coordinate halo roots `1/8,1/16,1/32,1/64`; it is used only when the observed cross rate exceeds `1/8`, making every removed cross term strictly smaller. Lower coefficient proofs repeat that rate check and request the full halo when needed.

If the leading cross field lies along one direction and its scalar Bernstein polynomial is strictly positive, bounded subdivision proves a uniform oriented normal. When the leading field vanishes on an annular boundary, a second proof checks each Bernstein coefficient sequence, including lower Jordan orders and lower exponential rates. Finitely many eventually positive coefficient directions converging to the same unit vector form a shrinking cone; nonnegative Bernstein weights preserve that cone. This proves uniform convergence wherever the nearby surface has a nonzero derivative cross product, without requiring a nondegenerate finite vertex jet. The ordinary four-face example uses six such lower-order coefficients.

A varying leading vector field, opposite actual annular values, or a different lower-order normal at an actual annular corner proves nonexistence of one oriented limiting normal on the queried face. If the sufficient Bernstein cone cannot decide, a complete algebraic germ decision uses the original full halo and every active positive spectral mode, including Jordan powers. Its report uses `method="exactAnnularGerms"` and `completeDecision=true`. A returned decision establishes existence or nonexistence; nonstationary exact masks and resource exhaustion remain explicit failures. No result asserts local injectivity, global orientation or absence of self-intersection.

The result attaches `constrainedNormalCertificate`. A successful native result has `normalMethod="exactConstrainedNormals"`. `normalCertified=true` and `normalExists=true` record a proved normal; `normalExists=false` records a proved geometric obstruction; an absent `normalExists` records an unresolved case. `decisionCertified` distinguishes the first two from unresolved outcomes. The report includes recurrence degrees, dominant-root multiplicity and isolator, the exact recurrence polynomial, halo-filter use, cell/coefficient-cone counts, exact-history step counts and allocation charges. Rationals use signed little-endian base-`2^24` limbs. `normalAvailable` remains separate: a proved normal may fail `normalTolerance` during native conversion and return `reason="normalPrecision"`.

Normal-proof defaults are `normalTolerance=1e-5`, `maxNormalKrylovDegree=128`, `maxNormalSubspaceDimension=128`, `maxNormalPrimes=128`, `maxNormalCells=10000`, `maxNormalDepth=16`, `maxRootDepth=128`, `maxRootNodes=100000`, `maxExactBits=8192`, and `maxExactCoefficients=20000000`. These counts bound the stated arrays, not bytes or elapsed time. The opt-in normal proof raises an omitted `maxWork` default to 500,000,000; an explicit `maxWork` always takes precedence. Degree, prime, bit, coefficient, work and root exhaustion throw. Larger sectors can require higher budgets.

## Complete limiting-normal decisions

The full annular fallback works in the ordered field of eventual exponential-polynomial germs. Each coordinate is expanded exactly as a sum of `lambda^k * k^j` with real algebraic coefficients. A common algebraic field contains every positive root of the verified coordinate recurrence. Nilpotent zero modes vanish after finitely many refinements. Exact spectral projectors and truncated inverses supply all Jordan coefficients. Root pairing and interval sign filters are accepted only after exact identity or outward-enclosure checks.

Products with equal algebraic growth rates are combined before signs are taken. The greatest nonzero growth rate, then the greatest Jordan power, determines the eventual sign. Formal polynomial GCD cancellation controls rational expression size; it does not assume that the exponential generators are multiplicatively independent. The denominator is proved nonzero as a germ before cancellation. This accounts for paths through parameter regions that shrink as refinement progresses.

A positive formal tolerance `epsilon` is smaller than every positive real constant and larger than every vanishing refinement germ. For a nonzero candidate direction `N`, choose `N[a] != 0`, set `D=J[a]*N[a]` and `Q[j]=J[j]*N[a]-J[a]*N[j]`. Uniform oriented convergence is equivalent to all four inequalities `epsilon*D +/- Q[j] >= 0` for the other two components, everywhere on each closed fundamental annulus. They enforce the correct orientation and vanishing relative transverse error. A zero Jacobian satisfies the inequalities harmlessly; a field that is eventually identically zero instead returns `normalExists=false, reason="noRegularPoints"`.

Full Collins projection and Thom sign determination decide these unit-square inequalities over a real closure of the germ field. Thom root sections avoid ordinary bisection in infinitesimally narrow intervals, and positive infinitesimals sample the open sectors. The general algebraic basis for real-closed-field quantifier elimination with Thom encodings is described by [Perrucci and Roy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02879). This implementation uses a bounded Collins decomposition with signed-remainder sign queries.

To relate the formal answer to ordinary limiting normals, eliminate the two parameter quantifiers. Only finitely many coefficient signs remain. For each such coefficient, its first nonzero growth/Jordan term has a rational-function coefficient in `epsilon`; its sign agrees with every sufficiently small fixed positive real tolerance, followed by sufficiently large refinement index. Taking the finite intersection preserves that agreement. The cone formula is monotone in tolerance, so truth at the formal tolerance is exactly uniform convergence at every real tolerance. Failure is an eventual obstruction at some positive real tolerance. Noncollinear leading coefficient vectors already prove varying directions; otherwise both orientations of their common line are checked.

`exactPolynomialNormals=true` supplies the analogous complete local decision for a degenerate analytic bicubic patch. It replays exact control histories, reconstructs regular or reflected feature controls, forms the exact polynomial derivative cross product, and substitutes `u=u0+/-delta*x, v=v0+/-delta*y`. Here `delta` is smaller than every positive expression in the slow tolerance. One corner quadrant, two edge quadrants, or all four interior quadrants cover the relative neighborhood of the queried face. Parameter reflections change the evaluation location, while the original derivative-cross orientation is retained. Boundary results set `oneSided=true`; they do not assert agreement with another face.

Successful polynomial fallbacks set `normalMethod="exactPolynomialNormals"` and attach `polynomialNormalCertificate` with `method="exactPolynomialNormal"`, `normalExists`, `normalCertified`, `decisionCertified`, tested quadrant count, leading order, allocation diagnostics and native precision information; the enclosing query reports its counted work. The finite derivatives retain their ordinary values, including zero or collinear vectors. Positional `complete` remains a separate claim. `Examples.SingularPatchNormal` represents `F=(u^3+3uv^3,v,-4.5u^2v^2)`: its first partial vanishes at the corner, but the normal tends uniformly to positive Z.

Either exact normal option defaults to `maxWork=500000000`, `maxExactCoefficients=20000000`, `maxExactBits=8192`, `normalTolerance=1e-5`, `maxNormalPolynomialDegree=128` (at most 512), `maxNormalTarskiQueries=100000`, `maxNormalProjectionPolynomials=256`, and `maxNormalCells=10000`. Existing recurrence/root limits still apply to constrained annuli. Query and cell budgets accumulate across each full proof. Coefficient, degree, work, root and cancellation failures throw; they never become a geometric nonexistence result. Native unit-vector enclosure may return `normalPrecision` after an exact existence proof. Expensive algebraic fields may require larger explicit budgets. No normal certificate claims injectivity, global orientation or freedom from self-intersection.

## Choosing data rules

The default remains face-local linear interpolation for intrinsic UV/color/alpha and the channel's declared interpolation for other corner attributes. Opt into smooth data through `options.faceVarying`. Each channel has its own connected data charts, so a UV seam need not split a color or scalar field.

```luau
local refined = E.Subdivision.catmullClark(cage, 3, {
    faceVarying = {
        uv = "corners",
        color = "smooth",
        ["attribute:temperature"] = "smooth",
    },
    faceVaryingSeams = {
        uv = { ["12:13"] = true },
    },
})
```

Channel tokens are `"uv"`, `"color"`, `"alpha"`, or `"attribute:" .. name`. Requested intrinsic fields must be finite and present at every face corner. Requested custom fields must have the corner domain and linear numeric interpolation: number, Vector2, Vector3 or Color3. Sparse values use the declared default. Categorical/min/max/nearest channels retain their ordinary transfer behavior when not requested; requesting them for this numeric interpolation rejects. Geometric normals are recalculated separately, and tangent-frame fields are not implicit face-varying selections.

The six modes add progressively stronger linear constraints:

| Mode | Data behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `smooth` | Catmull-Clark smoothing in chart interiors, cubic crease interpolation along chart boundaries. |
| `corners` | Additionally pins boundary chart vertices incident to one face. Other boundary vertices remain smooth. |
| `junctions` | Additionally pins a boundary chart vertex when three or more connected chart fans meet at its geometric vertex. |
| `concave` | Additionally pins boundary chart fans with more than two incident faces and darts where both chart boundary edges are copies of the same geometric edge. |
| `boundaries` | Pins every chart boundary vertex. Boundary edge values use endpoint midpoints; interior data remains smooth. |
| `linear` | Retains old corner values, uses edge midpoints and face averages everywhere. It can still record explicit seam topology. |

These categories follow the published [OpenSubdiv face-varying interpolation model](https://opensubdiv.org/docs/subdivision_surfaces.html#face-varying-interpolation-rules). The table above is this package's precise topological classification contract; concavity uses connected fan incidence, not angles in UV or world space. This is an independent implementation and does not claim bit-for-bit OpenSubdiv compatibility or its permissive nonmanifold extensions.

Geometric sharpness and boundary constraints take precedence. Smooth data inherits finite/infinite edge and vertex creases, including uniform/Chaikin decay and fractional transitions. A data seam supplies its own infinitely sharp chart boundary. `boundary="edgeAndCorner"` and `boundary="fixed"` impose the corresponding geometric vertex constraints on every data channel. Thus data cannot smooth across a geometric constraint that keeps its position sharp. The data rules affect values and data topology only; all modes produce the same geometric positions and polygon topology for the same geometric options.

## Charts and explicit seams

A manifold geometric edge is continuous for a channel only when values agree exactly at both endpoints, no explicit seam cuts it, and any stored data identities agree. Continuity joins the adjacent face-corner fans. Distinct geometric vertices are never welded merely because they have equal values. Disconnected fans stay separate. A seam ending inside a connected fan creates a dart; its two half-edges remain distinct even when their endpoint values coincide.

`faceVaryingSeams[token]` accepts an existing-edge boolean set or the name of a boolean edge attribute. Keys use the mesh's canonical `"smallerId:largerId"` format. True cuts an edge; false leaves continuity to its values and identities. Missing edges, nonboolean entries and incompatible channel schemas reject. An explicit seam token without a corresponding `faceVarying` mode uses `linear`. Selections refer to the input cage, and descendant seams propagate through subsequent levels. Reusing the same option table does not mutate its seam set.

Smooth chart vertices use the same geometric stencil structure: face averages, smooth or creased edge masks, smooth interior vertex masks, cubic boundary masks, and corner masks. Typed values are evaluated from deterministic source-corner stencils. Numeric interpolation preserves sparse defaults where the result equals the default; positions and other native vector/color fields retain their usual floating-point limits. This does not certify that UVs remain injective, avoid overlap, or achieve a particular texture distortion.

## Persistent data identity

Configured channels generate a reserved numeric corner attribute named `subdivision_fvar_topology:` followed by the channel token. For example, UV identities use `subdivision_fvar_topology:uv`, and a custom heat field uses `subdivision_fvar_topology:attribute:heat`. Each positive integer identifies one value in that channel's refined control topology. These are opaque labels, not geometric vertex IDs or interpolation weights. They allow a seam to survive when smoothing or authoring makes both sides numerically equal.

`Mesh:clone` and typed `IO.encode/decode` retain these attributes. Later subdivision calls process their identity topology even when the channel is omitted from `faceVarying`; omitted channels then use the default `linear` rule. Supply the desired smooth modes again to continue smooth refinement. Calls that have neither configured channels nor stored identity metadata retain legacy behavior and add no such attributes.

Editing values can introduce new discontinuities. Existing identities continue to separate previously distinct chart fans even if the values become equal. To deliberately rebuild a channel's connectivity from its current values, remove that channel's reserved topology attribute with `Attributes.remove`, then refine using the desired modes/seams. Reserved topology channels keep their numeric corner/nearest schema and zero default. Positive integer IDs retain existing identities; zero identifies newly created, unassigned data from other mesh builders. Adjacent unassigned corners can form fresh charts, while known and unassigned identities stay separate. Refinement assigns positive IDs to all output corners. Negative/noninteger IDs or conflicting schemas reject. If deleting a custom data field entirely, also remove its reserved topology channel.

Native EditableMesh conversion retains supported UV/color/alpha values and seams, but arbitrary typed attributes, including these identities, are not exported by the native adapter. Keep the authoring mesh or typed JSON for later subdivision, particularly for coincident seam values. `Examples.SmoothUVPanel` demonstrates explicit UV cuts and an independently smooth scalar field.

## Multires, budgets and cancellation

Multires construction and coarse `commit` use the same data interpolation modes. Original-cage seam selections are reapplied when rebuilding from the coarse level; finer levels use their retained identity topology. Coarse commits still require unchanged geometric topology. An error or cancellation leaves all stored levels unchanged. Position detail transport retains its existing local-frame behavior; this addition does not introduce a separate residual-detail solver for arbitrary corner attributes.

The existing defaults remain `maxVertices=250000`, `maxFaces=250000`, with levels from 0 through 16. Additional defaults are `maxFaceVaryingValues=1000000` and `maxFaceVaryingWork=20000000`. Before output construction, the per-level field/identity estimate is ten times the input face-corner count times the number of processed channels: input values and identities plus four child corners' values and identities. The work budget counts chart/stencil/value visits across all levels in a call. Each multires commit starts a new work budget. These estimates bound data processing; they are not byte or wall-clock guarantees.

`cancelled()` is checked before each level and during face-varying work. `faceVaryingCheckpoint({stage, work, channel})` runs at channel boundaries, completion and approximately every 256 counted visits. It may yield or throw. The existing `checkpoint(progress)` callback keeps its numeric level-progress argument. Budget exhaustion, invalid channel/topology input and cancellation throw without altering the caller's mesh. The usual manifold edge, connected vertex-fan and finite-data requirements apply.
