# Polygon editing and repair

`E.MeshEdit` provides region inset, individual extrusion, loop cuts, vertex slides, poke, triangle pairing, grid filling, spin/screw, plane bisect and finite knife paths. Every operation returns `(newMesh, report)` and leaves its source unchanged. Face selections are dictionaries keyed by existing face IDs; `true` and positive numbers select a face, while false/zero values do not. Surviving vertices and unmodified faces retain IDs. Replaced faces retain their IDs where described below; additional elements use the mesh's monotonically allocated IDs.

```lua
local E = require(game.ReplicatedStorage.Editable3D)
local mesh = E.Primitives.box(Vector3.new(4,2,3))
mesh = E.MeshEdit.insetRegion(mesh, {[5]=true}, 0.2, {depth=0.3})
local cut, report = E.MeshEdit.loopCut(mesh, "1:2", {0.25,0.75})
local raised = E.MeshEdit.extrudeFaces(cut, {[1]=true}, 0.15)
assert(raised:validate().closed)
```

Inputs must be valid, consistently wound polygon meshes with simple, approximately planar faces. Touched output faces are checked with exact projected predicate signs for polygon simplicity and triangulation, followed by topology/attribute validation. These checks reject crossed face boundaries, degenerate faces and invalid fans; they do not certify freedom from intersections between different faces. Nonplanar polygons remain polygonal approximations. Numerical scale is limited by native float32 vertex positions.

Derived vertex, face and corner data has explicit source correspondence. Typed channels use their declared interpolation mode; skin weights and groups follow vertex correspondence. Material slots are retained. Generated elements with no source correspondence, such as bisect caps and detached profile faces, receive channel defaults. A new edge segment derived from an old edge copies that edge's attributes; genuinely new interior/radial/vertical edges receive channel defaults. Intrinsic corner fields interpolate independently in each source face, retaining UV and custom corner seams. Normals are recalculated with the retained sharp-edge channel. Reports expose `maps`, `addedVertices`, `addedFaces`, `replacedFaces` and final `validation` in addition to operator-specific results.

## Region inset

`MeshEdit.insetRegion(mesh, selected, width, options)` offsets the selected region's boundaries inward by a positive width and adds a ring of side faces. Internal selected edges remain internal; adjacent selected faces do not each receive a separate border. Holes, disconnected regions and nonplanar connected patches are supported. Boundaries that touch ambiguously at a vertex reject.

At each boundary vertex, the new position satisfies the two boundary-edge distance constraints in the area-weighted regional tangent plane. This gives a miter offset on planar regions. `miterLimit=8` limits displacement divided by width. Near-reversing or incompatible boundary constraints reject. The optional `depth` (default zero) additionally offsets selected vertices along their regional normals; interior selected vertices retain IDs and boundary vertices are duplicated. With no selected boundary, width has no effect, while nonzero depth still offsets the selected shell. An empty selection returns an independent unchanged mesh.

For a planar source region (`planarTolerance=1e-5`), original and inset boundary loops are checked for intersections/touches, orientation reversal and departure from the selected face union. Depth is projected out for these checks. A shrinking outer loop and expanding hole that collide reject; this operator does not resolve offset events by changing topology. Nonplanar regions receive local face and topology checks, with `report.planarChecks=false`; they do not receive a global 3D intersection certificate. Reports also include `insetVertices[oldId]=newId`, `boundaryLoops`, `sides` and `maximumMiter`.

Duplicated vertices and their face corners retain their original data. New side faces copy the incident selected face's endpoints and material. This retains seam identity but leaves side-strip UV width zero unless the caller remaps those UVs afterward. It does not infer a new texture layout. `maxBoundaryVertices=2000` bounds boundary size; `maxWork=5000000` separately bounds planar intersections and classification.

## Individual extrusion and poke

`MeshEdit.extrudeFaces(mesh, selected, distance, options)` creates separate top vertices for every selected face and translates each top by its own face normal times a nonzero signed distance. Top face IDs remain unchanged. Side quads inherit the source face material and endpoint corner data. Reports provide `tops[sourceFace]` (new vertex IDs) and `sides[sourceFace]` (side-face IDs). Adjacent individual extrusions can have coincident or intersecting walls; this operation preserves their separate topology and does not perform a boolean union. Open sources retain an open base boundary.

`MeshEdit.poke(mesh, selected, options)` inserts a center vertex and one triangle for each source polygon edge. `center="median"` averages vertices; `center="area"` uses triangulated area-centroid weights. `offset=0` moves the center along the source normal. Attributes, weights and groups use the same center weights. The first triangle retains the face ID. Reports include `centers[sourceFace]` and `faceMap[sourceFace]`.

The center must lie strictly inside the polygon's projected visibility kernel so the triangle fan does not overlap or reverse. Concave polygons whose selected center lies outside that kernel reject. Use general triangulation for polygons without a suitable fan center. Nonzero offsets create a pyramid-like fan rather than planar triangulation.

## Loop cuts and vertex slides

`MeshEdit.loopCut(mesh, edgeKey, cuts, options)` follows the complete ordered quad ring through the seed edge. `cuts` is a count from 1–128 (default 1), or an increasing list of fractions strictly inside `(0,1)`. Fractions are oriented from the smaller vertex ID to the larger vertex ID of the ring's first ordered edge, then transported consistently through the strip. This first edge need not be the supplied seed when the ring is open; inspect the report's rails for the exact orientation.

Each traversed quad becomes several quads. The first band retains the source face ID. Open rings may end at a boundary or non-quad face; cuts are inserted into that terminal face's boundary as collinear vertices so no T junction is introduced. Closed rings synchronize the seam and reject a twisted endpoint correspondence. Source UVs, skin weights, groups and typed data interpolate at every edge split. Reports include the ordered `ring`, `rails[edgeKey]={a,b,vertices,parameters}`, `loops` of cut vertex IDs and `faceMap` of replacement bands. The ring's existing traversal budgets also apply; an incomplete traversal is never used as a complete cut.

`MeshEdit.slideVertices(mesh, targets, factor, options)` moves each keyed vertex toward its explicitly specified adjacent target vertex. `factor` lies in `[0,1)`. All positions use the original mesh snapshot, so simultaneous slides do not depend on dictionary iteration order. Vertex and corner data stays attached to its existing element; UVs are not projected again. Faces that invert relative to their original normal reject unless `allowInversion=true`; face simplicity and degeneracy checks always apply. To move an existing edge loop, use Selection's ordered loop result to supply a rail target for each vertex. It is an explicit rail selection API, not a heuristic automatic rail selector.

## Triangle pairing

`MeshEdit.trianglesToQuads(mesh, selected, options)` considers adjacent selected triangles with four distinct boundary vertices. Candidates must produce a strictly convex projected quad, have compatible materials and stay within `maxAngle` (default 30 degrees in radians) between face normals. Interior corner angles must lie between `pi-maxCornerAngle` and `maxCornerAngle` (default 165 degrees in radians). Candidates are sorted by squared departure from right angles and paired greedily with deterministic edge-key ties; each triangle participates once. This is not a globally optimal quad matching solver.

By default, nondefault data on the removed edge, differing typed face/corner channels and differing intrinsic corner fields at shared vertices delimit candidates. `attributeTolerance=1e-6` compares numeric/vector/color fields. `delimitAttributes=false` explicitly allows attribute merging: face and shared-corner values blend using source triangle areas and their declared typed interpolation modes; categorical ties follow deterministic source order. Material boundaries are always retained. The smaller face ID survives. Reports include `pairs`, accepted `candidates` and counts of `rejected` reasons. `maxCandidates=500000` bounds stored candidates. Nonplanar quads can have a different triangulated approximation from the original diagonal.

## Grid filling

`MeshEdit.gridFill(mesh, boundary, span, options)` fills one ordered even-length boundary with a rectangular quad lattice. If the boundary has `N` vertices, the grid dimensions are `span` by `N/2-span`, both positive integers. The four sides therefore have matching opposite vertex counts. `offset=0` rotates the starting boundary index before choosing corners. Existing loop edges must be boundary edges; consistently reversed input is automatically reoriented without changing the chosen corner partition. Loose ordered vertices are also supported. The input does not repeat its first vertex at the end.

Boundary positions and IDs remain fixed. Interior positions use a discrete Coons construction: blend the four sampled boundary rails and subtract their bilinear corner blend. This supports curved and nonplanar boundaries, but severe concavity or incompatible rails can fold the patch; local polygon and topology validation reject detected failures, without a global intersection certificate. Existing loose vertices inside the boundary are not reused automatically. Reports include the row-major `grid`, oriented `boundary`, `span` and `height`.

Interior vertex channels, weights and groups use a positive blend of the four corresponding rail samples: `(1-v)/2`, `v/2`, `(1-u)/2`, `u/2`. This is deliberately different from the signed geometric Coons weights. At boundary corners, source corner data comes from an existing edge incident on the new cell; outgoing-edge precedence resolves a corner adjacent to two different source faces. Interior corner data blends the same rails, using each rail vertex's first indexed source corner. New face channels average contributing source faces, and material comes from their first face unless overridden by `material`. `uv="grid"` generates unit-grid UVs; the default preserves/interpolates source UVs and generates grid UVs only for faces with no source faces.

## Spin and screw

`MeshEdit.spin(mesh, profile, origin, axis, angle, steps, options)` rotates an ordered vertex profile about a finite nonzero axis. Angles are radians. Every step also translates by `rise/steps` along the normalized axis (`rise=0`). `MeshEdit.screw(mesh, profile, origin, axis, turns, pitch, steps, options)` sets the total angle to `turns*2*pi` and total rise to `turns*pitch`; `steps` is the total number of sweep intervals, including for multiple turns. Turns and angles may be negative. Each angular interval must be strictly smaller than pi in magnitude.

`closedProfile=true` joins the last profile vertex to the first. An exact full turn within `1e-12` radians, with zero rise, welds its final ring to the input IDs unless `merge=false`. More than one zero-rise rotation rejects. With zero rise, vertices exactly on the axis are shared poles and neighboring quads become triangles; `axisTolerance=0` can be increased explicitly to snap near-axis vertices onto the axis. Nonzero-rise profiles do not collapse poles. Non-pole source vertices retain their IDs in the first ring. Reports provide `rings`, the oriented `profile`, `poles` and `merged`.

Existing profile edges may attach an open sweep to boundary edges; orientation is adjusted consistently. A welded full turn cannot attach to an existing face edge. `cap=true` closes both ends of a detached closed profile when the final ring is not merged. Source profile topology determines winding; a detached profile can therefore produce an inward shell. Use `MeshRepair.orient` if positive-volume outward winding is required. Sweep collisions, overlap between turns and nonlocal self-intersections are not resolved automatically.

Vertex data is copied from the profile, ring edges inherit source-edge channels, and side face channels/materials inherit an incident profile face when present. Longitudinal edges use defaults. Profile corner data uses the first indexed incident corner; generated normals are recalculated and intrinsic tangents rotate with their ring. Typed Vector3 channels are generic values and are not implicitly rotated. Default UV behavior copies source UVs, potentially creating zero-width texture strips. `uv="sweep"` explicitly generates angular/profile-distance coordinates with separate corners at both seams; detached profiles receive those UVs by default. Generated end caps use planar UVs when no source corners exist or sweep UVs are requested. `material` overrides generated face materials; `maxSteps=10000` limits intervals in addition to shared allocation limits.

## Plane bisect

`MeshEdit.bisect(mesh, origin, normal, options)` cuts the complete mesh with a plane. `keep="both"` (default) retains both halves sharing the cut vertices. `keep="positive"` or `"negative"` retains only that signed half. `cap=true` requires an initially closed source and one retained side. Caps support disconnected loops, concavity, annular holes and nested even/odd domains. Intersecting/touching loops, branching boundaries and incompatible nested shell orientations reject. Coplanar source faces are retained, except that capping removes detached entirely coplanar components into `discardedCoplanarFaces` so a tangent cut does not invent a zero-volume closed shell.

Uncut faces retain their polygons and IDs. A straddling polygon is triangulated, each triangle is clipped, and the first retained fragment keeps the source face ID. This intentionally introduces some triangulation edges. Concave faces can produce disconnected retained regions; triangles entirely on the plane are emitted once. Nonplanar polygons use their triangulated piecewise-linear interpretation. The `faceMap` lists fragments of cut or discarded source faces; `caps`, `capLoops`, `intersections[sourceVertexPair]=newVertex`, `planeEdges` and `cutTriangles` describe the result. `planeEdges` includes every resulting edge whose vertices were classified on the plane, including edges of existing coplanar faces. Unused vertices are removed.

Signed distances use scalar arithmetic on native coordinates. `tolerance=0` classifies only computed zero distances as on-plane; positive tolerance snaps vertices in that absolute distance band onto the plane. Constructed intersections use a canonical source-edge direction and are shared across faces. They are rounded to native float32; a cut collapsing onto an endpoint rejects and requests an explicit tolerance. The plane construction is numerical, not an exact algebraic intersection certificate. `maxCutTriangles=500000` bounds processed source triangles. Cap triangulation also uses Planar's vertex/work limits (default 2000 boundary vertices and 5000000 work units; `maxVertices` overrides both the shared mesh and Planar limit).

Vertex/skin/group data interpolates along original edges or internal triangulation diagonals. Each clipped face retains its source face and corner channels, keeping UV discontinuities. Split original edges inherit their channels; internal cut/triangulation edges use defaults. Cap face/corner channels use defaults, material is `material` or 1, and intrinsic UVs are planar coordinates in a deterministic frame about `origin`.

## Finite knife paths

`MeshEdit.knife(mesh, strokes, options)` splits planar polygons along finite, optionally bent paths. A stroke is `{face=faceId, path={start, ...interiorPositions, finish}}`. Endpoints are topological descriptors: `{corner=i}` uses the source face's one-based corner index, while `{edge=i,t=fraction}` interpolates along its directed edge from corner `i` to `i % cornerCount + 1`, with `t` strictly inside `(0,1)`. Interior positions are native Vector3 values. They must be strictly inside the face and within `planeTolerance=1e-5` of its plane, then are projected onto it.

```lua
local detailed, report = E.MeshEdit.knife(E.Primitives.box(), {
    {face=5, path={{edge=1,t=0.25}, Vector3.new(0.3,1,0), {edge=3,t=0.75}}},
})
assert(detailed:validate().closed)
```

Every path runs from boundary to boundary. It may bend inside a concave face, but must not cross/retrace itself, leave the face, touch another boundary point or follow a boundary edge. Exact projected signs classify segment intersections and region winding. One path per source face is accepted per call; compose calls using reported `faceMap` IDs for further cuts. Free interior endpoints, automatic screen/ray picking and simultaneous crossing stroke arrangements are not implemented by this API.

Shared edge intersections use a canonical parameter direction; coincident native positions or parameters within `1e-12` reuse an existing cut vertex. Every incident face receives the same edge vertices even when it has no stroke. Each selected face becomes two oriented polygons, with the first retaining its source ID. Interior data uses barycentric interpolation in a containing source triangle; boundary data interpolates separately per incident face, preserving seams. Source split-edge attributes are retained and new path edges use defaults. Reports include `paths[faceId]`, sorted `edgeCuts[edgeKey]`, `faceMap` and `work`. `maxPathPoints=2000` bounds all stroke points; `maxWork=5000000` bounds segment comparisons and interpolation searches, alongside shared polygon/allocation limits.

## Cleanup and winding

`MeshRepair.clean(mesh, options)` removes malformed face references/repeated IDs, zero-area faces, zero-length edges' incident faces, duplicate faces regardless of winding, nonfinite vertices, and loose vertices. It retains the earliest duplicate face and prunes references in typed channels, weights and groups. `areaTolerance=1e-12` is an absolute area threshold. `removeLoose=false` retains finite isolated vertices. `maxElements=2000000` bounds traversal/allocation, and `checkpoint` supports cancellation. The source remains unchanged.

The cleanup report lists `removedFaces[faceId]=reason`, `removedVertices`, `validation` and `complete`. `complete=false` means other topology issues remain, such as disconnected fans sharing one vertex. Cleanup does not weld nearby vertices, fill holes, correct winding or resolve general self-intersections. Use the explicit weld/fill/orientation operations as appropriate.

`MeshRepair.orient(mesh, options)` propagates winding constraints through edge-connected components. Edges with more than two faces and nonorientable constraint cycles reject. Closed components are made positive-volume by default; `outward=false` preserves the orientation chosen from each component's lowest face ID. Open components use that seed orientation. Corner channels, intrinsic corners and tangent signs follow any reversal; normals are recalculated. Reports include the flipped face set, component faces/closure/volume, topology validation and `complete`. `maxWork=2000000` limits corner visits. Signed-volume orientation assumes an embedded consistently wound shell; it is not a nesting-aware solid classifier and does not remove intersections.

## Budgets and verification

Mesh-edit defaults include `maxVertices=500000`, `maxFaces=500000`, `maxElements=2000000` for connectivity, `maxFaceVertices=2000` and `maxFaceWork=5000000` for each touched polygon validation. Limits may cause an explicit error before those upper limits when another constraint is exhausted. `checkpoint` can yield or throw; shared connectivity/planar stages supply a numeric progress value, while the builder supplies `{stage,vertices,faces}`. Allocation/validation/cancellation errors leave the source unchanged and create no native instances.
